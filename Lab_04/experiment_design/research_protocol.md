# Research Protocol – Student Task Manager

## Study objective
Compare a traditional single-page task form (Interface A) with a guided task-creation wizard (Interface B).

## Consent briefing
“Thank you for agreeing to participate in this usability study. Today, you will evaluate two designs of a Student Task Manager application. You will create and save an academic task in each design.

We are evaluating the software interfaces, not your intelligence, computer skills, or personal abilities. There are no right or wrong ways to approach the interface. Mistakes help us identify parts of the design that may need improvement.

Your participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time without penalty. We will record task completion time, errors, and your ease rating. Your results will use a participant code rather than your name. We will use fictional task information, not real academic records.

Screen recording, if proposed, will only occur with your separate permission. Do you have questions? Do you voluntarily agree to participate?”

Obtain consent before testing. Follow course or institutional research requirements; do not claim formal ethics approval unless granted.

## Standard task instruction
“Imagine that you are a college student who needs to add a new assignment to your task manager. Using the information provided, create a task with the specified title, description, course, due date, and priority. Save the task and verify that it was successfully created. Please work as you normally would. I can clarify the task instructions, but I will not guide you through the interface.”

## Session procedure
1. Prepare the participant’s assigned order (AB or BA) and two equivalent scenarios.
2. Confirm the standardized browser, display, keyboard, and mouse.
3. Read the consent briefing and obtain consent.
4. Ask the same familiarity questions of each participant.
5. Read the task scenario exactly as written.
6. Start timing at the start cue.
7. Observe without coaching.
8. Stop timing when successful save is verified, or at the five-minute limit.
9. Record completion time, errors, success, assistance, and observations.
10. Ask: “Overall, how easy or difficult was this task?” Record 1 (Very difficult) to 7 (Very easy).
11. Provide a two-minute break.
12. Repeat using the other interface and the alternate equivalent scenario.
13. Debrief the participant.

## Researcher rules
- Do not point out controls or suggest actions.
- Do not express approval or disapproval.
- Count incorrect entries, rejected submissions, and participant corrections consistently.
- Record assistance, interruptions, and technical issues.
- Do not pause timing during ordinary interaction.
- If a technical failure prevents a fair test, document it and follow a consistent repeat procedure.

## Timing and task success
Allow approximately 15–20 minutes per session. A successful task is saved and visibly verified. If the participant cannot complete the task within five minutes, record success = 0 and time = 300 seconds; note any assistance or interruption.

## Counterbalancing
Use six participants total: three in AB order and three in BA order. Balance the two equivalent task scenarios across interface and order, and record the scenario actually used in the data sheet.

## Privacy and accessibility
Use participant IDs, fictional task information, and restricted-access storage. Collect only necessary data. Obtain separate permission for recording. Check keyboard access, visible focus, form labels, contrast, semantic structure, and understandable validation feedback. Provide reasonable accommodations where possible and document them.
